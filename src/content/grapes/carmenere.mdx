Carmenère was a Bordeaux blending grape, wiped out by phylloxera in the 1860s and presumed dead for over a century. It survived in Chile, misidentified as Merlot until 1994, when ampelographers finally called it. That accident gave Chile its identity grape — and gave Carmenère time to adapt to a climate that suits it better than Bordeaux ever did.

In the glass: deep ruby, aromas of blackberry, dried chili, and unmistakable green bell pepper when yields run high. Tannins are medium and smooth; acidity is moderate. Pair with ribeye charred over wood — tannin meets protein. Lamb shoulder with cumin works the same way. Avoid delicate fish; this grape bulldozes subtlety.
