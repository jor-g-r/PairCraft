Glera is the grape behind Prosecco, a fact Italy made legally binding in 2009 when it locked the name to the Conegliano Valdobbiadene zone and mandated Glera as the primary variety. Before that, the wine and the grape shared the name Prosecco — a branding disaster the DOC rules finally fixed. It is a workhorse grape with one job: deliver clean, high-acid, low-phenolic base wine that takes Charmat-method bubbles without fighting them.

In the glass it is lean, pale, and direct — green apple, white peach, a whisper of almond. Acid carries everything. Pair it with prosciutto e melone: salt meets fruit, bubbles scrub fat. Order it with fritto misto di mare: acid cuts oil. Do not age it. Do not overthink it. Open it cold, drink it fast, order another.
