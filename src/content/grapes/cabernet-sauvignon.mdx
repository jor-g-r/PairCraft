A natural cross of Cabernet Franc and Sauvignon Blanc, Cabernet Sauvignon escaped Bordeaux in the 19th century and colonized every serious wine region on earth. It dominates Napa, anchors Maipo, and stiffens Super Tuscans. It earned that dominance: thick skins mean color, tannin, and structure that outlast almost every other red grape in the cellar.

In the glass: blackcurrant, graphite, cedar, and a grip that demands protein. Pair it with ribeye charred over hardwood — tannin meets protein, fat softens the grip. Aged manchego, 24 months, works the same way. Avoid delicate fish or anything acidic enough to turn those tannins metallic. Young Cab needs a plate; old Cab needs nothing but a clean glass.
