INRA bred Marselan in 1961 by crossing Cabernet Sauvignon with Grenache, chasing disease resistance and small berries. It spent decades as an obscure Languedoc blending tool before China adopted it wholesale — today more Marselan grows in China than anywhere else on earth, and it is the country's most planted quality red variety.

In the glass: deep violet, concentrated dark fruit, firm tannin from Cabernet, and Grenache's warmth underneath. Tannin meets protein — go straight to lamb shoulder braised with herbs, duck confit, or a charred ribeye. The structure holds up to spice better than pure Grenache, so Sichuan peppercorn lamb skewers are not a stretch. Avoid delicate fish; this wine will bury it.
