Pinot Meunier is the most-planted grape in Champagne and the least talked about. It buds late, ripens early, and thrives in the frost-prone Vallée de la Marne where Chardonnay and Pinot Noir struggle. Négociants leaned on it for decades without crediting it; now a handful of growers — Egly-Ouriet, Jérôme Prévost — bottle it alone and dare you to dismiss it.

In the glass it delivers red apple, brioche crust, and a round mid-palate that softens Champagne's acid spine. Fruit meets acid — that is the mechanism. Pair it with roast chicken thighs, a croque-monsieur, or fried oysters dusted in cornmeal. Blanc de Meunier is the move with anything fried: fat meets fruit, acid scrubs the palate clean.
