Albariño thrives on the Atlantic edge of Galicia, where salt-laden wind
and granite soil push it toward something Spain produces almost nowhere
else: an aromatic white with the cut of cool-climate acidity.

The hallmark is contrast — ripe peach and citrus pulled tight by salinity
and a chalk-like grip. It belongs with anything from the sea, which is
the lunch you eat in Galicia anyway.
