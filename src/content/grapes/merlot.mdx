Merlot built the right bank of Bordeaux — Pomerol and Saint-Émilion run on it. It ripens early, tolerates cooler clay soils, and became the world's shortcut to approachable red wine after the 1990s boom. That popularity nearly killed its reputation. Serious Merlot never went away; it just got buried under oceans of soft, jammy commodity wine.

In the glass: dark plum, black cherry, cocoa, sometimes a iron-blood edge on cooler sites. Tannins are rounder than Cabernet, acid moderate. Drink it with duck confit — fat meets fruit — or lamb shoulder braised with olives, where the fruit amplifies the meat's richness. Avoid delicate fish; Merlot's weight crushes them. At its peak, in Pomerol or a serious Tuscan blend, it is one of the world's great red grapes.
