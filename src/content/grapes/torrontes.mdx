Torrontés is Argentina's answer to the question nobody in Europe thought to ask. Three distinct biotypes exist — Riojano, Sanjuanino, Mendocino — but Riojano grown at altitude in Salta's Calchaquí Valleys is the one worth chasing. It descends from a cross of Muscat of Alexandria and Criolla Chica, which explains everything about its personality: Muscat's floral fireworks grafted onto a dry, high-acid frame.

In the glass: white peach, rose petal, orange blossom — then a clean, almost austere finish that stops the aromatics from becoming cloying. Acid cuts through fat. Pair with ceviche de corvina, spiced lamb empanadas, or Thai green curry with jasmine rice. Serve cold, drink young, and stop looking for oak.
