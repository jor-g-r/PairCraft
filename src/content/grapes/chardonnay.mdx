Chardonnay originated in Burgundy and conquered every serious wine region on earth within fifty years. It is the grape of Chablis, Meursault, and Montrachet — and also of ten thousand forgettable supermarket bottles. That range is the point: no white grape absorbs terroir and winemaker intent more faithfully, which makes it either thrilling or tedious depending on who's in the cellar.

Unoaked, it runs lean and mineral — pair it with oysters on the half shell or poached turbot, acid meeting brine. With full malolactic and new oak, it turns broad and buttery — put it against roast chicken with pan drippings or seared scallops with brown butter, fat meeting fat. Aged Burgundy, ten years in, earns roasted bone marrow or aged comté. Read the bottle before you order; Chardonnay is not one wine.
