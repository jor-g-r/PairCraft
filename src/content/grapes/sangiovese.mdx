Sangiovese has been the spine of Tuscany since at least the 15th century. It is the grape behind Chianti, Brunello di Montalcino, Vino Nobile di Montepulciano, and Morellino di Scansano — four distinct expressions from one variety, shaped by altitude, clone, and winemaker nerve. It resists shortcuts: thin skins demand sun, late ripening demands patience, and premature bottling punishes everyone.

In the glass: sour cherry, dried herbs, iron, and a finish that dries the back of your teeth. Acid cuts through fat; tannin meets protein. Pair with bistecca alla Fiorentina charred over oak, wild boar ragù on pappardelle, or aged pecorino. Avoid delicate fish — Sangiovese will flatten it. This grape does not flatter; it challenges, and the right food answers back.
