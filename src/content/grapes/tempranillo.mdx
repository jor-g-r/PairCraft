Tempranillo is Spain's answer to Cabernet and Nebbiolo — a grape with enough structure to anchor a country's wine identity. Rioja made it famous; Ribera del Duero made it serious. Centuries of oak aging shaped its personality: this is a grape that does not resist the barrel, it converses with it.

In the glass, expect dried cherry, leather, tobacco, and cedar — fruit that runs savory rather than sweet. Tannins are firm but not brutal; acid is moderate. Roast lamb with herbs is the canonical match, tannin meeting protein. Aged manchego, 24 months, works on salt meeting fruit. Avoid delicate fish — this grape overwhelms anything without weight.
