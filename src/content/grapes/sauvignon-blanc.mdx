Sauvignon Blanc originates in the Loire Valley — Sancerre and Pouilly-Fumé are its benchmarks — and spread globally faster than almost any other white grape. Bordeaux blends it with Sémillon for weight and age-worthiness. New Zealand turned it into a category-defining phenomenon. California rebranded it Fumé Blanc to sell it. None of that changes what it is: a high-acid, aromatic white with a strong point of view.

In the glass: grapefruit pith, cut grass, green capsicum, sometimes jalapeño, sometimes wet stone. Acid is the engine. That acid cuts through goat cheese — fresh chèvre especially — slices fat in pan-seared branzino, and locks onto the herbaceous notes in chimichurri-dressed skirt steak. Oysters on the half shell are a near-perfect match: salinity meets citrus. Avoid heavy cream sauces; the acid turns aggressive with nowhere to go.
