Pinot Noir has been grown in Burgundy since at least the 4th century. It mutates constantly, clones number in the hundreds, and it exposes every winemaking decision without mercy. No grape is more transparent about where it grew and who made it. That is its power and its trap.

In the glass: low tannin, high acid, red fruit shading to earth and dried rose as it ages. Pale garnet that never gets darker. Pair it with duck breast, seared; the acid lifts fat and the fruit mirrors the meat's sweetness. Salmon, roasted, works on the same logic. Aged pinot — five years minimum — belongs next to mushroom risotto or roast pigeon; umami meets umami, and the wine wins.
