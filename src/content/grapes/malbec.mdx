Malbec was a blending grape in Bordeaux until frost and disease pushed it
to the margins. It found its second life in Mendoza, where altitude, sun,
and the cold descent off the Andes produced wines deeper and more
perfumed than Cahors ever managed.

The signature: violets and dark plum, tannin that polishes rather than
grips, and just enough acid to make the fruit feel alive instead of jammy.
Drink it young, with red meat or anything off a grill.
