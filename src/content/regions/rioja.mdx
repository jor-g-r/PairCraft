Rioja sits in northern Spain along the Ebro River, divided into three sub-zones — Alta, Alavesa, and Oriental. Alta and Alavesa produce the wines worth chasing: higher elevation, thinner soils, sharper acidity. The region has been bottling and exporting longer than almost anywhere in Spain, which means the infrastructure is serious and the track record is real.

The stylistic signature is American oak vanilla layered over dried red fruit — Crianza, Reserva, and Gran Reserva classifications tell you oak time, not quality, so learn the producers. CVNE, López de Heredia, Muga, and Artadi define the range from classical to modern. Rioja Oriental runs hotter and fleshier; avoid it unless you want extraction over finesse.
