Champagne sits 145 km northeast of Paris, so cold that ripening is never guaranteed — that tension is the point. Roman-era chalk pits (crayères) run beneath the vines, locking in moisture and driving the piercing acidity that makes traditional-method bubbles here structurally different from anywhere else on earth. The Champenoise codified the méthode champenoise, and the grandes maisons have enforced its standards for 300 years.

Non-vintage Brut is the house style: blended across years, consistent, built on reserve wines. Blanc de Blancs from the Côte des Blancs (Cramant, Le Mesnil-sur-Oger) is pure Chardonnay — bone-dry, citrus-driven, acid cuts through anything fatty. Blanc de Noirs from the Montagne de Reims runs deeper, darker. Grower Champagnes (Selosse, Lassaigne, Bérêche) show single-terroir precision the big houses cannot match.
