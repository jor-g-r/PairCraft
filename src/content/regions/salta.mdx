Salta sits in Argentina's northwest, with its core in the Calchaquí Valleys. Vines at Cafayate and Cachi climb to 3,300 meters — the highest commercial viticulture on the planet. Volcanic and alluvial soils, brutal sun, and cold nights define every bottle. This is not an accident of fashion; indigenous winemaking here predates the Spanish.

The style is taut and aromatic: Malbec loses Mendoza's plush weight and gains grip, violet intensity, and a saline mineral edge. Torrontés — not on the grape list but the region's calling card — is irrelevant to skip here. Among sub-zones, Cafayate delivers the most consistent results; Cachi is wilder and worth the risk. Clos de los Siete has no footprint here — look for Colomé, El Esteco, and Tacuil.
