The Central Valley runs 400 km south of Santiago and contains four sub-valleys — Maipo, Rapel, Curicó, Maule — each with distinct soil and altitude. Maipo sits on ancient alluvial gravel over limestone; Maule is old-vine País and Carignan country on granite. The Andes to the east and the Coastal Range to the west create a natural corridor that makes irrigation-free viticulture possible in ways most New World regions cannot claim.

Maipo delivers structured Cabernet Sauvignon with tobacco and dark fruit; Colchagua (within Rapel) produces dense, warm Carménère. Maule is the sleeper: dry-farmed bush vines producing Carignan and Cinsault with real tension. Concha y Toro, Almaviva, and Antiyal anchor the prestige tier; Gillmore and Louis-Antoine Luyt define the Maule old-vine movement.
