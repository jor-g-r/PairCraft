Chile is a natural laboratory: the Atacama to the north, Antarctica to the south, ocean to the west, mountains to the east, and phylloxera kept out by all of it. Vines here are old, ungrafted, and stressed in ways European winemakers pay consultants to simulate. The Central Valley built Chile's export reputation on Cabernet; the coastal Casablanca and San Antonio valleys rewrote it on Sauvignon Blanc and Pinot Noir.

The action now is in the extremes. Itata and Bio Bio in the south produce Cinsault and País from dry-farmed bush vines that drink like nothing else on the continent — low alcohol, high tension, dirt under the fingernails. Maipo Alto still delivers the benchmark Cabernet. Elqui and Limarí in the north push Syrah and Chardonnay into mineral territory. Concha y Toro, Montes, and De Martino set the floor; Garage Wine Co. and Louis-Antoine Luyt set the ceiling.
