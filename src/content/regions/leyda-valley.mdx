Leyda sits 15 km from the Pacific in the San Antonio province, carved out as its own DO in 2002. Before irrigation arrived in the late 1990s, this was dry scrubland; water access unlocked some of Chile's coldest viable vineyard land. Soils are clay-heavy with granite outcrops — slow-draining, forcing roots down and concentrating flavor without inflating sugar.

Sauvignon Blanc here is the benchmark: green herb, flint, and citrus pith with acid that doesn't quit. Pinot Noir runs lean and red-fruited, closer to Côte de Beaune than to Casablanca's softer style. Viña Leyda, Matetic, and Garcés Silva (Amayna label) set the standard. Avoid anything labeled 'reserva' that doesn't show vintage date — freshness is everything here.
