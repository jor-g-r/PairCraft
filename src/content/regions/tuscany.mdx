Tuscany is central Italy's backbone — rolling hills, cypress lines, and two thousand years of vine culture. The key terroir split is altitude and soil: galestro (friable schist) drains fast and stresses vines productively; clay-heavy valley floors make fatter, blunter wine. Chianti Classico sits at the heart, Montalcino to the south, Montepulciano southeast, Bolgheri on the coast.

The stylistic signature is high acid, firm tannin, and a savory, iron-tinged core — wine that demands food. Brunello di Montalcino (Biondi-Santi, Soldera, Cerbaiona) is the benchmark for age-worthy Sangiovese. Chianti Classico Gran Selezione (Fontodi, Isole e Olena, Montevertine) delivers precision at a fraction of the price. Bolgheri (Sassicaia, Ornellaia) runs Cabernet-dominant and plays a different game entirely — richer, more international, still worth knowing.
