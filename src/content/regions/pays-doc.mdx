Pays d'Oc is the IGP blanket covering most of Languedoc-Roussillon — a vast arc of sun-hammered garrigue stretching from the Rhône delta to the Pyrenees. It exists because Languedoc needed a commercial identity for varietal wines outside the AOC straitjacket, and it worked: this is now France's largest IGP by volume, drawing on limestone plateaus, schist hillsides, and gravel plains.

The style is direct: ripe fruit, honest weight, no pretension. Reds run warm and dark — Cabernet and Marselan dominate the export shelves. Whites, especially Sauvignon Blanc and Chardonnay, punch above their price. Watch producers in the Hérault and Aude departments; the best are farming old bush vines and charging half what Burgundy asks for comparable concentration.
