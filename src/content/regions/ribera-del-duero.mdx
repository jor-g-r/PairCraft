Ribera del Duero runs along the Duero River through Castile, a windswept plateau where vines struggle and concentrate. The region earned DO status in 1982 but the vineyards are ancient; some Tinto Fino bush vines predate the designation by a century. Limestone and clay soils over river terraces do the structural work.

The wines are dark, tannic, and fruit-dense — blackberry and graphite, not the dried-fruit softness of warmer Spanish reds. Crianza, Reserva, and Gran Reserva tiers still matter here; oak is a tool, not a mask. Vega Sicilia sets the ceiling, Pingus redefined prestige in the 1990s, and Pesquera proved the plateau could produce serious wine at scale. Buy Reserva for the table; buy Gran Reserva for the cellar.
