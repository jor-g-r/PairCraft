Maipo sits at Santiago's doorstep, the oldest and most storied wine zone in Chile. The Andes loom east, pushing cold air down at night; the coastal range blocks Pacific fog to the west. Alluvial fans from Andean meltwater deliver gravel and sand — the same logic as Bordeaux's left bank, different hemisphere. This is where Chilean wine staked its international reputation in the 1980s and has been defending it since.

The style is dark-fruited, firm-tanninned Cabernet Sauvignon with a graphite spine that separates it from warmer-climate versions. Alto Maipo — Pirque and Puente Alto — is the address that matters: higher elevation, cooler nights, tighter structure. Concha y Toro's Don Melchor and Almaviva come from here. Viña Aquitania and Casa Real push the benchmark further. Drink these wines with ribeye, charred — tannin meets protein, full stop.
